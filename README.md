# Closing the Logistics Gap: LEADS 2025 State Logistics Policy Brief

**An independent consulting-style policy brief on what India's higher-performing states do differently, what the LEADS 2025 results reveal, and a practical 12-month reform roadmap for states seeking to improve logistics performance.**

Prepared by Harsh Kumar | November 2026

> **Important:** This is an independent portfolio case study. It is not an official Government of India, DPIIT, NCAER or State Government publication, nor an official assessment of any state. The analysis synthesises public information. The roadmap and KPI recommendations are the author's own. State policy incentives remain subject to each policy's notified eligibility conditions and implementation status.

## Executive summary

The central argument is that **a notified logistics policy is only the starting point**. States improve their logistics ecosystem when policy is translated into a funded action plan, clear ownership, land and infrastructure readiness, predictable approvals, digital services and structured industry feedback.

The brief is designed for a State Industries / Logistics Department and answers four questions:

1. What does LEADS 2025 say about the current spread of logistics performance across States and UTs?
2. What policy and institutional practices are visible in selected higher-performing states?
3. Where does the implementation gap remain?
4. What can a state do in the first 12 months, and how should progress be tracked?

## Key evidence used

The updated deck uses the official DPIIT LEADS 2025 report and related primary sources. Headline facts used in the presentation include:

- **India's logistics cost:** 7.97% of GDP, or approximately ₹24.01 lakh crore, for FY 2023–24, as estimated in the DPIIT–NCAER *Assessment of Logistics Cost in India*.
- **LEADS 2025 performance tiers:** 4 Exemplars, 11 High Performers, 17 Accelerators and 4 Growth Seekers across 36 States and UTs.
- **LEADS 2025 methodology:** 58.9% weight on objective indicators and 41.1% on perception indicators.
- **Policy adoption:** 28 States/UTs had notified a logistics policy; 18 of those had a logistics policy action plan.
- **Industry-linked benefits:** 32 States/UTs had extended industry-linked incentives and benefits under the broader LEADS 2025 indicator. This measure includes industry status, priority/thrust-sector classification, policy inclusion or notified provisions; it should not be interpreted as 32 States/UTs having formally granted identical industry status.

### Selected state examples

- **Tamil Nadu:** Its 2023 Integrated Logistics Plan contains 50 interventions across six strategic themes. The LEADS 2025 state profile gives an estimated investment outlay of ₹62,541 crore over the 10-year plan horizon and identifies TIDCO as the nodal agency.
- **Uttar Pradesh:** The Warehousing & Logistics Policy 2022 identifies UPSIDA as nodal agency. The official Invest UP policy summary provides differentiated incentives, including a 25% capital subsidy for eligible logistics parks, subject to specified caps and conditions.
- **Kerala:** Government Order G.O.(Ms) No. 74/2025/ID dated 8 October 2025 approved the Kerala Logistics Policy 2025. LEADS 2025 reports a 500+ policy-backed project pipeline and describes support for logistics parks and mini-MMLPs.
- **Rajasthan:** The Logistics Policy 2025 was notified on 19 February 2025 and published in the Gazette on 28 March 2025. Rajasthan is classified as a Growth Seeker in the LEADS 2025 landlocked group. The deck treats this as a policy-to-outcome illustration, not proof that the new policy caused the tier result.

## Recommended reform playbook

The brief proposes five linked levers, mapped to LEADS assessment areas:

1. **Institutions:** Establish or empower a State Logistics Cell, define nodal officers and create an inter-departmental review cadence.
2. **Costed plan:** Convert the policy into a sequenced action plan with projects, budgets, owners, dependencies and dates.
3. **Land and infrastructure:** Identify land for logistics/trucker parks and address first- and last-mile gaps through integrated planning.
4. **Digital services:** Improve single-window approvals, service timelines and appropriate state integration with national logistics platforms such as ULIP.
5. **Industry feedback:** Hold regular shipper and transporter forums and track resolution of recurring operational constraints.

## 12-month implementation roadmap

| Phase | Timing | Illustrative outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnose | Months 0–3 | Peer-group benchmark; stakeholder consultations; Logistics Cell and nodal officers; signed-off baseline |
| Design | Months 3–6 | Costed action plan; confirmed incentive rules; identified logistics / trucker park land parcels; approved governance |
| Deliver & track | Months 6–12 | Single-window improvements and ULIP integration plan; first project awards; monthly MIS dashboard; quarterly industry review |

## Methodology and important caveats

- The LEADS 2025 four-tier classification is not directly comparable with the 2024 Achiever / Fast Mover / Aspirer framework. Cross-year movements in the deck are therefore described as **directional category movement**, not like-for-like rank changes.
- The LEADS 2025 report states that objective-indicator evidence was submitted up to December 2025. The presentation is dated November 2026 but does not represent a live update of every state policy or project after that assessment window.
- The 32-State/UT industry-linked benefits measure is broader than formal industry-status notification. Its definition is described in the LEADS 2025 report.
- The former September 2025 National Logistics Policy progress release reported 27 policies and 14 action plans. The deck uses the later LEADS 2025 report's 28 / 18 figures as the more recent assessment snapshot.
- The national logistics cost estimate is for FY 2023–24 and should not be presented as a current-year estimate for FY 2026–27.
- The state examples are selected illustrations, not a causal evaluation of which policy caused a higher LEADS tier. Incentive availability, caps and eligibility must be checked against current government notifications before being used for investment decisions.
- **Kerala policy-status note:** LEADS 2025 describes logistics-park incentives and mini-MMLP development in the state profile, while the publicly posted 2024 Logistics Parks Policy page on Invest Kerala is labelled as a draft. The ₹7 crore / stamp-duty terms are therefore attributed to LEADS 2025 and should be checked against the latest operative government order before any real project or investment decision.
- The 12-month roadmap and KPI scorecard are proposed advisory recommendations, not official DPIIT guidance.
